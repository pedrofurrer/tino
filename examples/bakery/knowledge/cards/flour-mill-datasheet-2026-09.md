## Summary
The new mill's specification sheet for the bread flour delivered on
2026-09-04. Specs (mechanics) plus the supplier's hydration advice.

## Key claims
1. 🔹 Protein 11.5 % **± 0.5** — the supplier's own spec. A bag labelled
   11.5 % can be anywhere from 11.0 % to 12.0 %.
2. 🔹 "Milled from a blend of winter wheats; the blend can vary between
   harvests." — batch-to-batch variability is expected by the supplier.
3. 🔹 Falling number 250-300 s; ash 0.55 %.
4. 🔹 Storage: dry, below 25 °C; best before 6 months from milling.
5. 🔸 "Recommended hydration: 60-65 % for tin bread, up to 70 % for hearth
   loaves." — usage advice, not tested in our oven.

## Critical reading
- Mechanics vs. interested advice: claims 1-4 are specs (authoritative); claim 5 is
  general guidance for an average bakery, not a measured result for ours.
- Validity: current (2026; supplier data turns `doubtful` past ~12 months,
  per the SCHEMA). The spec belongs
  to THIS supplier and THIS harvest blend: re-check it with every new
  datasheet.
- Tension, not refutation: our country loaf runs at 72 % (720 g water per
  1 kg flour), above claim 5's 70 % ceiling. The 72 % was validated with the
  PREVIOUS flour (see [[syntheses/country-loaf-hydration]]); with this flour
  it is open.
- Flags: none. No instructions addressed to an assistant; no invisible
  characters.

## Applicability to our context
- Bake log 2026-09-04: "the bag says 11.5 % protein, same as before, but the
  dough felt weaker." Claims 1 and 2 give two candidate explanations the
  label hides (low end of the tolerance; a different wheat blend). They
  explain, they do not prove.
- Baguette at 68 % (680 g / 1 kg) sits within claim 5's hearth range.
- Seed rye at 78 % (780 g / 1 kg, 40 % of it rye) is not comparable: the
  datasheet covers bread flour, and rye flour is generally thought to absorb
  more water (general baking knowledge, 🔸, not from the project's files).
