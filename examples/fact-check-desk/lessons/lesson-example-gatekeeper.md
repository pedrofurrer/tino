> Illustrative EXAMPLE (anonymized, from a real case in another project). It
> serves as a reference for the format and the level of abstraction. When the
> project has 2+ lessons of its own, it can be archived.

**Trigger**: I am about to draft (or propose) a request, an appeal or a
procedure whose success depends on the discretionary decision of a third
party with veto power — a platform's support desk, a bureaucracy, a supplier
who can say "no".

**Original error**: the agent proposed asking for a change by arguing its
merit ("it is due because the conditions call for it"). That framing invited
the third party to JUDGE whether the change was due — a judgment that had
already been lost once — and brought the request closer to the platform's
sensitive policies. The user spotted the better framing.

**Principle**: phrase the request as the minimal technical operation whose
execution does NOT require the third party to assess merit or intent: make
saying "yes" administrative routine and saying "no" take effort. Do not
reopen judgments already lost. Use the third party's own statements as
leverage (quote them, not your argument).

**Application**: instead of "approve this change because it is due" (merit),
ask "correct the leftover record that keeps the already approved operation
from taking effect" (technical integrity), quoting what the platform itself
stated in earlier exchanges.
