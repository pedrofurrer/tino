**Trigger**: the concrete situation, recognizable BEFORE acting, that must
fire this lesson. If you cannot formulate it ("when I am about to do X…"),
this is not a lesson: record it in the project's logbook and move on. Write
it by MECHANISM — the ACT you are about to do + the INPUT that sets it off —
and leave the concrete forms as examples ("they illustrate, they do not
limit"): a trigger that lists the surface where the error was born does not
fire when the same error arrives through another surface. If it recurs
through a form that was not listed, REFORMULATE it by mechanism (keeping the
history of formulations in the card); do not add the form to the list.

**Original error**: what was done or proposed, why it was wrong, and how it
was found out (user correction / data / reality). With the date and the
case. What the correction rests on goes in `evidence`: if it is only the
user's word, the lesson does not go beyond `recurring` until data, a primary
source or an observed outcome corroborates it.

**Principle**: the distilled rule, as an actionable imperative. Neither the
one-off fact (it does not transfer) nor a generic maxim like "think better"
(it does not discriminate): the level of abstraction that lets you recognize
and act.

**Application**: what applying the principle well looks like — ideally the
direct contrast with the original error.

verifier counts them and the introspection measures the recurrence rate):
