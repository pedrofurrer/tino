## Verdict text (proposed for publication)

**Mostly false.** Enrollment in Port Alder's public school district has
fallen every year since 2020-21, the first year in the data the desk
downloaded. But
the drop is about 4 percent, not 15. From the 2020-21 school year to
2025-26, the district went from 18,450 students to 17,700: 750 fewer, a
decline of 4.1 percent.

The 15 percent figure does appear in a district document, but not as a
measurement. The district's 2019 facilities plan projected enrollment to
2025 under three scenarios. The most pessimistic one foresaw "a decline of
up to 15 % by 2025, if birth rates keep falling and no new family housing is
built." The plan's baseline scenario was a decline of about 5 percent. The
actual decline so far is closer to the baseline than to the worst case.

Neither the claim as logged nor any document we hold gives a period or a
source for the figure. Whatever
period is meant, the city's data from 2020-21 on do not show a decline
anywhere near 15 percent.

## The arithmetic (every figure from `data/school-enrollment.csv`)

Enrollment per school year (students), city open-data portal, downloaded
2026-09-15:

| School year | Students | Change vs. previous year |
|---|---|---|
| 2020-21 | 18,450 | — |
| 2021-22 | 18,320 | −130 (−0.70 %) |
| 2022-23 | 18,100 | −220 (−1.20 %) |
| 2023-24 | 17,960 | −140 (−0.77 %) |
| 2024-25 | 17,820 | −140 (−0.78 %) |
| 2025-26 | 17,700 | −120 (−0.67 %) |

- Whole series: 18,450 − 17,700 = 750 students; 750 ÷ 18,450 = 0.0407 →
  **−4.1 %**.
- To 2024-25 (the plan's horizon, "by 2025"): 18,450 − 17,820 = 630;
  630 ÷ 18,450 = 0.0341 → **−3.4 %**.
- Claimed vs. measured: 15 ÷ 4.07 ≈ 3.7 — the claim overstates the measured
  decline about 3.7 times.
- What a 15 % fall would require: ending at 17,700, the starting point would
  have to be 17,700 ÷ 0.85 ≈ 20,824 students — 2,374 more than the earliest
  year in the data (18,450). The largest single-year drop in the data is
  1.20 %.

## Limits of this check (what we do not know)

- The data start in 2020-21. They cannot show enrollment in 2019 or earlier,
  so a 15 % fall from some earlier year is not ruled out by the data —
  though it would need about 2,400 more students before 2020-21 than the
  series shows, against a trend of roughly 1 % a year or less.
- We do not know where the mailer's figure comes from. The 2019 plan's low
  scenario is a plausible origin; it is not proven to be the origin.
- The comparison with the plan is approximate: the plan counts from 2019,
  the data from 2020-21.
