# Example: one user's hard rules

*Leer en español: [es/principles-example.md](es/principles-example.md).*

**This is example output, not a default.** tino installs none of these
rules. They are what the author's own cross-project lessons became after
months of use in 2026: lessons that were confirmed, graduated with the
author's OK and rewritten without any reference to the project they came
from (see *graduation* in the [guide](guide.md#metacognition)). Yours will be
different, because they will come from your corrections.

1. **No metadata or traces in delivered files.** Anything that leaves for a
   third party (a document, a spreadsheet, an image, an export) carries no
   metadata and nothing that reveals how it was made: author, user,
   machine, company, application and version, templates, editing times,
   local paths or the tools used. Audit every file of a delivery, including
   inside containers: office formats hide the author, templates and local
   paths in internal parts. If a trace cannot be removed without changing
   what the reader sees, say so instead of calling the file clean.

2. **The date of the data is part of the data.** Before deciding, stating or
   delivering something that rests on a claim about a state, ask two
   questions: does it describe something that changes? Is my evidence from
   today, and from the primary source? If it changes and was not checked
   today, check it first or label it "unverified". This covers outside
   states (pages, catalogs, consoles, third-party docs), your own data and
   metrics with an earlier cutoff, and state claims in your own documents,
   which are as old as their last edit. Every deliverable with numbers
   carries a visible cutoff; if the delivery is postponed, the check is
   repeated. Reproducing a number does not prove it still holds.

3. **Provenance travels with the recommendation.** Every recommendation
   (a number, a threshold, a price, an order, a sequence, a structure)
   carries its origin in writing when it is made: my own assumption, said
   in those words; a source's claim; consensus; or verified with my own
   data. A number without a label passes for a fact one session later.

4. **What is derived does not inherit the rank of what is primary, and the
   unit of verification is the piece.** A summary (a form field, a person's
   sentence about their own data, a tool's extract) compresses with
   systematic loss: before quoting it, recording it or building on it,
   check it against the primary source or measurement at hand, or label it
   "uncorroborated". When verifying a set (a table, a list, a batch), each
   row carries its own source or its label: being next to verified rows
   does not verify a row, and the piece that best fits the story deserves
   the most scrutiny. A sentence inside a text is not necessarily its
   signer's: before attributing it, establish who says it and what the
   signer did with it.

5. **A correction ends in what was derived, not at the origin.** When a
   claim, a figure, a rule, a tool or a method is corrected, the correction
   is not closed until everything derived from it has been reviewed: copies
   of the text, documents born from it, documents that apply the rule, and
   the results the tool or method produced while it was wrong, including
   copies another system made on its own. Sweep by literal text, not by
   topic; first what goes to third parties; and review on purpose the
   document born from the error, which is the one attention skips.

6. **A result is judged as its recipient receives it.** Before declaring
   that something is done, works, is published or is clean, look at it in
   its final form, as the person who uses it receives it: the result opened,
   not the sign that the process ran (an exit code, a cost, a file that
   exists); the surface that answers the exact question, not a neighboring
   one; the region that changed, against a reference the process does not
   touch. If a channel copies, caches or re-derives on the way, trace what
   is actually served. What was not checked this way is declared as such.

7. **Your own source is opened first.** Before stating, evaluating,
   designing, comparing or analyzing something whose input lives in your
   own sources (your assets, your records, your earlier reports, your
   database, your own doctrine), open them in the session. They are the
   cheapest sources and the least consulted, because what is yours "is
   already known"; the standard of verification for them is higher, not
   lower. Claims about identity, names or numbering are looked up in the
   record before being written; in a comparison, your own row is filled
   first and from the primary source; an analysis starts with a search of
   earlier reports and records; a claim of absence requires the total count.
